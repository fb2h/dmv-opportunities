What this is

A directory of work opportunities for high school students in DC, Maryland and Virginia — paid or unpaid: internships, volunteer positions, paid jobs, apprenticeships, research positions, job shadowing, and counselor or trainee roles with real duties. It also lists genuinely accessible online positions.

It exists because the lists that were already online were maintained by hand, refreshed once or twice a year, and mixed paid internships with expensive summer camps as if they were the same thing. The organizations that offer these positions mostly run them every year; what changes is the dates. So this directory is built to remember each program's history, check the official pages for changes, and refresh itself with very little human attention.

What it deliberately leaves out

Anything a student attends rather than works at: summer camps, enrichment classes, dual enrollment, early-college and magnet programs, test prep, tutoring, college fairs, information sessions and leadership seminars. The simplest test: if you pay to attend, it is not a work opportunity and it is not here. Stand-alone scholarships and competitions are also out of scope.

The rules the data follows

Nothing is guessed. If the organization's page does not state a date, fee, age or eligibility rule, the listing says "not stated." A program that "usually opens in November" is never given a November deadline — a pattern is not this year's fact.
Only announced cycles are shown as current. A program whose page still shows last year's session is not listed as if it were open this year. Its history is kept in the database and tells the system when to start checking for the new announcement.
Every fact traces to the organization's own page. Each listing shows the exact sentences it was built from, with a link. Third-party lists and blog roundups are used only as leads to check, never as sources.
Dates keep the precision the source gave them. "February" stays "February"; it does not become February 1. "Mid-October" stays "mid-October."
Pay filters are strict. "With pay" means an explicit wage or guaranteed stipend on the page. "No pay" means the page explicitly says unpaid. A page that says nothing about money matches neither — it is labelled "Pay: not stated."

How the filters work

FilterWhat it does
Distance from ZIPStraight-line miles between your ZIP area and the program's listed site, not driving time. Sites are placed at ZIP or town level, so treat distances as approximate. Programs whose work site is assigned after acceptance, or whose address is not published, appear under Conditions to confirm with no distance.
ParticipationIn person, online or hybrid, as the page states it. Online positions are kept regardless of distance unless you choose "In person."
Current gradeYour grade now. Where a page says "rising juniors," the directory converts; where the conversion is ambiguous the listing is shown under Conditions to confirm.
When offeredSummer, school year, or year-round. Year-round positions match every choice.
PaySee the strict rule above.
Field of interestNine broad fields; a position that plausibly fits two carries both, so either selection finds it.

Things that are shown in details but are deliberately not filters: age, citizenship or work authorization, residency and school restrictions, essays, recommendation letters, interviews, fees, and application status. These are shown on each listing instead, because they proved too unreliable to filter on without hiding programs from students who qualify.

"Matches" versus "Conditions to confirm"

A listing under Matches your filters satisfies every filter you set with facts the page actually states. A listing under Conditions to confirm fails none of your filters, but one condition is not stated on the page or is ambiguous — for example, the page does not say which grades are eligible. The directory shows these rather than hiding them, because a student who sees an extra program loses one click, while a student who never sees a program loses the opportunity.

Where the data comes from

Each program was checked against the hosting organization's own website, PDF or formal notice. The directory currently draws on several hundred verified source pages across federal agencies, county and city governments, school districts, hospitals, parks, libraries, museums and non-profits in DC, Maryland and Virginia. Some official sites block automated checking; those programs are being verified by hand and added as they clear.

The whole database and the rules that govern it are public. The data is stored as plain text files, one per program, in a public repository, so every change is recorded and reversible.

Who made this

This is a student's non-profit project, started in 2026 by a high school student in Montgomery County, Maryland, who could not find a reliable list when looking for a summer internship and decided to build one that would still work the following year without being rebuilt. It was developed with the help of an AI assistant. It is free and carries no advertising.

Always confirm details on the official page before applying. This directory reports what organizations publish; it does not represent them.
